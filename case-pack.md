## C04 — The delivery arrived. The invoice tells a different story

Day 1 / Octopus. The receiving team needs to reconcile delivery evidence,
shortages and a later invoice.

### Your client

Dealership parts receiving lead. *The following is fictional role-play dialogue,
not a real client quote.*

> At the loading bay we need to record what actually arrived. Later, someone tries
> to reconcile the invoice. They cannot tell whether a difference was a shortage,
> a damaged item, a duplicate scan or a second delivery.

### Your starting question

**What should the employee record at receipt so the next person can resolve the
difference?**

You have `initial.json`. Ask the client about the workflow before deciding what
to build.

## Working agreement

This is a fictional exercise inspired by real workflow patterns. The records,
dialogue and rules are synthetic. You are free to challenge the first proposed
solution. Discover who needs what decision, then choose a narrow workflow.

Use the harness and tools provided by the event. Tool access must not become the
task. If a service is unavailable, use a labeled local simulation. Disclose
generated or hard-coded outputs. A scripted animation is not evidence that an
agent performed an action.

Expect a change of information during the exercise. Show a trigger, an
evidence-backed proposal, a person who can approve or correct it, and the
resulting state. A button that explicitly simulates an incoming event is
acceptable. Call it a simulation.

### Exercise boundary

All documents are structured synthetic records; scanned-document input can be
simulated and must be labeled. No supplier message, inventory update or
accounting entry is actually executed.

Any additional simulated records you create must be labeled and kept separate
from the supplied test records. Do not alter the supplied records to make your
prototype appear correct.

---

## What to hand over

- A working prototype or clearly labeled clickable demonstration with a
  repeatable start state.
- A short record of the pain, client feedback and what you changed after feedback.
- An evidence view and at least one uncertain or failure case.
- A short README: run instructions, real vs simulated components, limitations and
  the next validation test.
- A Wolf handoff identifying the next integration, access requirements, owner and
  unresolved risk.

Show the result first in your presentation. State which promise you can
demonstrate and which remains a hypothesis. Do not contact real customers or
publish your prototype as an official client service.
